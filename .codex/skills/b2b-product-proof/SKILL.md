# B2B product proof

Convert technical capability into an offer a buyer can assess quickly. Do not fabricate users, savings, compliance, or testimonials.

## Position from evidence

Use only demonstrated functionality, test results, deployment evidence, and clearly labelled assumptions. Write:

1. **Buyer:** role and team that owns the pain.
2. **Pain:** a costly, recurring operational failure.
3. **Outcome:** the state the buyer gets, in their language.
4. **Proof:** demo, screenshot, installation path, test, or documented safety behavior.
5. **Boundary:** what the product deliberately does not automate.

Avoid selling a framework name or generic "AI agent." The offer should still make sense if the model or orchestration library changes.

## Build the conversion path

Offer one clear next step: a short audit, a paid pilot, or a demo. A good pilot names the team, scope, timeframe, success measures, and stop condition. Keep the first conversion path short: product page, proof, pilot scope, contact route.

## Evidence checklist

- a live or recorded demo that starts from a believable customer problem;
- a public README that states setup, permissions, data handling, and limitations;
- a pilot document with objective success criteria;
- screenshots or a concise video showing the primary workflow;
- a support and privacy route before inviting production use.

End with the weakest evidence in the funnel and the single smallest action that would strengthen it.
