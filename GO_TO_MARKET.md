# Go-to-market: Sunset

This plan sells a defined operational outcome before it attempts self-serve SaaS growth. It is an operating hypothesis, not evidence of existing demand.

## Positioning

**For** engineering, platform, and delivery teams using Jira, **Sunset** keeps temporary workarounds from becoming invisible permanent debt by recording an owner, exit condition, evidence, and human review. Unlike a generic AI agent, it does not autonomously alter Jira records: it surfaces permitted evidence and makes the accountable review easy to complete.

## Ideal first customers

- Jira Cloud teams of roughly 25–250 engineers with platform or delivery ownership.
- Regulated, security-conscious, or enterprise-adjacent teams that need visible approval and auditability.
- Teams with recurring temporary flags, migration exceptions, manual support steps, or compatibility workarounds.

Avoid teams with no named owner for the process, no recurring workaround problem, or an expectation that software will replace accountability.

## Offer ladder

| Offer | Scope | Suggested starting price | Proof of value |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Workflow-debt audit | One project, sample of active decisions, 60–90 minute readout | €500–€1,000 | Clear review queue and governance gaps |
| Sunset pilot | One project, 3–10 decisions, two weeks | €2,000–€4,000 | One concrete before/after outcome and expansion decision |
| Team rollout | Additional projects, onboarding, governance configuration | Quote after pilot | Measured adoption and sponsor-approved scope |
| Marketplace subscription | Self-serve only after repeatable pilot demand | Test after three comparable pilots | Installation, conversion, retention |

Prices are hypotheses. Keep the audit and pilot paid: a free pilot attracts curiosity but does not establish willingness to pay.

## Customer acquisition channels

### 1. Warm outreach and targeted outbound

Start with engineering leaders, Jira administrators, and platform leads in your existing network. Then contact a small number of companies that publicly show Jira Cloud usage and active platform or delivery teams. Every outreach message should reference a visible, relevant operational context and offer the fixed-scope audit—not a generic "AI automation" pitch.

Use a simple sequence: a concise first note, a follow-up with the 90-second demo, and a final close-the-loop note. Stop after three touches unless the prospect responds. Never use scraped private data or send mass templated messages.

### 2. Atlassian-native distribution

Publish useful technical material in the Atlassian Developer Community and attend relevant Atlassian Community Events (ACE). Share an audit checklist, lessons from permission-safe Forge implementation, and a small public demo. The long-term distribution path is the Atlassian Marketplace, but do not list before the external install path, support route, and pilot evidence are ready.

### 3. Partner channel

Approach small Atlassian consultancies and Jira administrators who already run migrations, governance, or service-management engagements. Offer Sunset as a narrowly scoped add-on that helps them leave the customer with an ongoing review process. Partners are valuable only when the use case is repeatable; avoid bespoke resale promises.

### 4. Proof-led content

Publish one practical piece each week:

- a short video of the workflow from workaround to human review;
- an anonymized checklist for auditing temporary Jira exceptions;
- a concise engineering note on idempotent task creation or permission-safe evidence;
- after a real pilot, an approved case study with measured results.

GitHub is proof for technical evaluators, not the primary lead-generation channel. LinkedIn and the Atlassian community are better for reaching economic buyers; Marketplace is the discoverability channel once the offer is mature.

## First 30 days

1. Publish a stable demo and this pilot scope.
2. Prepare a one-page landing page with the outcome, video, pilot scope, privacy/support route, and one contact method.
3. Hold 15 discovery conversations; ask about real workaround examples and the cost of missing reviews.
4. Sell two paid pilots before adding broad integrations or autonomous features.
5. Turn the clearest pilot outcome into a case study, with customer approval.

## Metrics that matter

Track conversations booked, qualified pilots, paid pilots, pilot-to-rollout conversion, time to first configured decision, weekly active reviewers, and decisions reviewed before their deadline. Do not use GitHub stars, agent calls, or raw installs as success metrics unless they correlate with paid adoption.
